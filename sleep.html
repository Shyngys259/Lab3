Sleep and Technology

Technology is an important part of our daily life, but it is also important to create time for rest.

Sleep is the first thing sacrificed and the last thing noticed. The damage is rarely dramatic — it shows up as a slow drift: ten minutes later to bed, a little harder to fall asleep, a little more tired in the morning. Two mechanisms are at work. Bright light close to your eyes delays the signal that tells your body the day is over, and stimulating content keeps your attention engaged when it should be winding down. Neither is solved by willpower at midnight; both are solved earlier in the evening.

Healthy Sleep Habits

  • Stop using your phone before sleeping.
  • Keep your phone away from your bed.
  • Use relaxing activities before sleep.
  • Keep a regular sleep schedule.

The Last Hour Before Bed

A wind-down routine does not have to be long, it has to be the same every night.

  • Phone charges outside the bedroom.
  • Lights dimmed, screens off or warm-toned.
  • Something slow: reading, stretching, a shower.
  • Same bedtime window, weekends included.

Why the Phone Belongs Elsewhere

Keeping a device within arm's reach keeps it in play — for the alarm, for one last check, for the notification that wakes you at three in the morning. Move the charging point to another room and the decision is already made for you.

An inexpensive alarm clock is one of the highest-return purchases available to anyone who scrolls in bed.

Sleep problems that persist are a medical matter

If you have tried a clean routine for several weeks and still cannot sleep, or you feel exhausted despite adequate hours, speak to a doctor. Persistent insomnia, snoring with pauses in breathing, and daytime sleepiness have causes that habit changes alone will not reach.
