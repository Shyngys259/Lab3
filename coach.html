Frequently Asked Questions

How much screen time is actually too much?

There is no universal number. The useful test is displacement: if your screen time is eating into sleep, movement, study or time with people you care about, it is too much for you at this stage of your life. Start by measuring a normal week, then set a target slightly below it — not at zero.

Do I need to delete social media completely?

Usually not. Most people get further with a structured middle ground: keep the apps that genuinely add something, remove or restrict the ones that reliably leave you feeling worse, and give yourself defined windows rather than unrestricted access. A month-long break is a useful experiment if you want to find out what you would actually miss.

My phone is my alarm — do I have to move it?

A cheap standalone alarm clock solves this in one purchase and removes the most common excuse for keeping a phone within reach overnight. If that is not an option, put the phone on the far side of the room, face down, on silent, with the charging cable short enough that you have to get out of bed to reach it.

How long does it take for new habits to stick?

Longer than the popular figure suggests, and it varies by habit and person. Expect the first week to be uncomfortable, the second to be easier, and a lapse somewhere in the first month. A lapse is not a failure of the plan; it is part of it. What matters is restarting the next morning rather than writing off the month.

When should I get professional help instead?

If your technology use is causing real harm — lost work or study, broken relationships, or a mood that stays low — talk to a doctor, a counsellor or a mental-health service. Persistent insomnia and symptoms of anxiety or depression deserve professional attention, and habit advice is not a substitute for it.

Where to Get Help

If what you are dealing with goes beyond screen habits, these are the people to talk to: your family doctor or GP, a school or university counsellor, and your local mental-health service or crisis line. In an emergency, contact your local emergency number straight away.
