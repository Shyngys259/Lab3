Focus and Attention

The cost of a distraction is not the minute it takes — it is the time spent getting back to where you were.

Every interruption has two prices. The first is the interruption itself. The second, much larger one, is the recovery: rebuilding the mental thread you were holding before the buzz. When a phone sits face-up on the desk, a share of your attention stays on it even while you are working, waiting for it to do something. Moving it out of sight removes that tax without any effort of will.

Five Ways to Protect Concentration

  1. Put the phone in another room

    Out of sight is the single most effective step. Face-down on the desk is a distant second.

  2. Work in blocks, not stretches

    Twenty-five minutes of single-task work, five minutes off. The block is short enough that starting feels cheap.

  3. Silence everything but people

    Turn off every notification except calls and messages from real contacts. Badges and banners are what pull you back.

  4. Keep one tab, one document

    Background tabs are visual noise. Close what you are not reading right now.

  5. End the day on purpose

    Note where you stopped and what comes next, so tomorrow starts without a decision.
