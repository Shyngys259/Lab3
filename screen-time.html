Screen Time

Screen Time

Too much screen time can take away time from studying, sleeping, exercising and communicating with other people.

The number itself matters less than what it displaces. An hour of video calls with a friend and an hour of short-form video are the same on the counter and completely different in effect. So the useful question is not "how many hours did I log" but "what did those hours replace". If the honest answer is sleep, movement, or time with people, that is where to start — not by deleting apps, but by taking back the specific block of time they are sitting on.

Useful Tips

  • Take regular breaks from your screen.
  • Turn off unnecessary notifications.
  • Set a daily screen-time limit.
  • Spend more time on offline activities.

How to Measure a Baseline

  1. Look at last week, not today

    Most phones show a seven-day average. A single day tells you nothing; the weekly pattern shows where the hours actually collect.

  2. Find the top three apps

    Almost always three apps hold most of the total. Everything else is noise.

  3. Note the time of day

    Heavy use usually clusters in two windows — right after waking and just before bed.

  4. Write down one number

    Pick a target for the coming week and put it somewhere visible. A number you can remember beats a setting you forget.

Change one thing at a time

Cutting five habits at once lasts about four days. Cutting one habit and holding it for two weeks is how the change survives a bad week.
